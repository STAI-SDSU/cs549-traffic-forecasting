# Rules

## Teamwork

Students work in groups of four or five.

Sharing code, trained models, predictions, or project solutions **between
groups** is prohibited. Each group's submission must be its own work.

## External Resources

You may consult:

* research papers;
* official library/framework documentation;
* publicly available implementations;
* pretrained models;
* generative AI tools, if permitted by the course's general academic
  integrity policy.

**Any external code, pretrained model, or AI-assisted development must be
clearly disclosed and properly attributed in the final report.**
Undisclosed use of external code or AI-generated code that is not your own
work is an academic integrity violation.

## Test Data

You must not attempt to recover, infer, search for, or otherwise obtain the
hidden test labels.

Using publicly available versions of the METR-LA dataset to understand the
data (e.g. reading about its general characteristics, or referencing the
original paper) is allowed. You must **not** use any externally obtained
ground-truth data to reconstruct this course's instructor-defined hidden
evaluation set, look up its labels, or otherwise bypass the leaderboard.

## Leaderboard

The **public leaderboard** is visible during the competition and is
intended to help you sanity-check your submission format and get a rough
sense of standing while developing your model.

**Final leaderboard grading is based only on the private leaderboard**,
which is not revealed until after the submission deadline. Do not tune your
model specifically to the public leaderboard at the expense of general
performance — see the grading rubric in [`README.md`](README.md).
